# DEPARTMENT CHARTER — Design / Product
## Division 5. The taste layer — and the guardian of "Apple-simple, not Apple-locked-down."

**Status:** founding charter, for the founder/board. Authored by the CEO function.
**Why fifth:** it is real but not load-bearing for safety or correctness — so it
sits behind the adversary, the prover, and the explainer. But it is the division
that protects a founding promise that is *easy to lose under engineering pressure*:
Elpis should "run on a toaster," be **"Apple-simple but not Apple-locked-down,"**
and **put the user above all else.** Left undefended, a device-control OS drifts
toward either developer-only complexity or vendor-style lock-in. This division
holds the line that it stays simple *and* free.

---

## 1. MANDATE
Own coherence and taste across everything a human touches — the Elpis shell, the
device-control UX, the installer experience, the brand ("elpis" lowercase Teko,
"Hope begins in the dark"), and the product principles that keep the whole thing
*for the user*. Make it feel like one considered thing, not an assembly of lanes.

Its product is **a coherent, humane, un-captured user experience** — simplicity
that never becomes a cage.

---

## 2. WHAT IT OWNS
- **The product principles as a standing check:** runs-on-a-toaster (resource
  humility), Apple-simple-not-locked-down (no dark patterns, no lock-in, no
  user-hostile defaults), user-above-all (the user's interest wins ties). A feature
  that violates these gets flagged the way Security flags a trust-boundary change.
- **UX coherence** across the shell, device controllers, and installer (the
  familiar Windows/macOS/Ubuntu home paradigm, the Pandora layer as first-class
  desktop citizen) — so the parallel lanes don't produce a patchwork.
- **The brand & identity** — the elpis mark, the palette (void/protocol-blue/
  semantic tiers), the visual voice; the Orrery's design language too.
- **Product direction inputs** — what's worth building from a *user-value* stance,
  feeding the EVALUATE/sprint-priority conversation alongside the threat-informed
  inputs.

## 3. THE LINE IT HOLDS
- **It never overrides Security or QA.** Beautiful and simple never beats safe or
  verified. A design preference is not a reason to widen a trust boundary or skip a
  gate. (Taste serves the constitution, exactly as the Visualizer-vs-correctness
  ordering does.)
- **Simplicity is never achieved by removing the user's control or freedom** —
  that's the Apple-*locked-down* failure mode, and it's explicitly out of bounds.
  Simple means *fewer ways to get confused*, never *fewer things the user is allowed
  to do*.
- It **proposes**; UI that touches the installer/trust surface still routes through
  REVIEW (Lane I install-mechanics) and Security; the founder disposes of anything
  public-facing.

## 4. RELATIONSHIP TO THE OTHER ORGANS
- **Development** builds what it specs; **Lane I (Installer)** is its closest
  partner on the install experience (I owns mechanics+UX, Design owns the coherence
  bar over it).
- **Security/Red-Team & QA** outrank it on every tie — safe and verified first,
  then humane and beautiful.
- **DevRel** shares the "honest, non-hyped voice" standard; brand consistency
  spans both.
- **CEO function** coordinates; **Founder/board** is the ultimate arbiter of taste
  *and* the presser of any public button.

## 5. ANTI-IDLE BACKLOG
Tighten shell/controller/installer UX coherence; maintain the design system and the
elpis brand; pressure-test features against the three product principles; refine the
Orrery's design language; propose user-value-driven priorities for the next sprint.

## 6. NOT
Not above Security or QA (safe/verified beats pretty/simple, always). Not a license
to remove user control in the name of simplicity (that's lock-in). Not a publisher
of public-facing material (founder + the relevant flags). Not the final arbiter of
direction (founder is).
