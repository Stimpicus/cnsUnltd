# VISION — what it can look like

A world where real-time command dispatch is **common infrastructure no one owns** —
the way TCP/IP is no one's property — running on cheap hardware everywhere, owned
by the people using it rather than the companies mediating it.

- Elpis on a $35 board in a kitchen, a workshop, a classroom — not a subscription,
  not a walled garden, not a surveillance endpoint. The user's device, serving the
  user.
- The protocol as a substrate other people build on, on substrates we never
  imagined, because we proved it was substrate-agnostic and then opened it.
- The safeguards — the consent-by-default, the write-authorization-proportional-
  to-effect, the disclosed attack class — as the reason this became useful
  infrastructure instead of a control nightmare. The elpis at the bottom of the box.
- An open-source community that improved the thing past anything its two founders
  could have reached, because disclosure invited them and the patents protected the
  commons from enclosure.

We will know it worked when the project no longer needs us — when the world has
taken it further than we could, and the safeguards held.

*Hope begins in the dark.*
